\begin{abstract}
We study products of fixed strictly positive matrices when every window of
$k$ factors contains at most $m$ occurrences of a designated miss mode.
For fixed dimension and $m$, a uniform cluster-packing argument identifies
the first-order logarithmic growth correction as the largest Perron-weighted
gain of a cluster with at most $m$ misses. The argument covers all admissible
words. A realizable finite-gap cutoff then gives certified two-sided bounds
at finite $k$, an effective accuracy threshold, and an $O(\log k/k)$ error
bound for the scaled coefficient. We formalize the main law and the
finite-window implications in Lean, with explicit correspondence between
the mathematical and formal definitions. Examples explain why no
dimension-independent list of pair gaps suffices and why the lower-bound
periodic witnesses need not be finite-window optimizers.
\end{abstract}

implications have kernel-checked Lean proofs. The automatic minorization-tail

\section{Introduction and scope}

A sliding-window loss rule controls local bursts, not only average loss
frequency. We ask how worst-case product growth changes when the window
becomes large while the matrices and the allowed number of misses remain
fixed. Periodic products supply lower bounds, but the central difficulty is
a matching estimate uniform over every admissible word.

The key observation is combinatorial. Join nearby misses into clusters and
assign each cluster to a bin determined by its first miss. With bin length
$k-mq$, the original $k$-window rule bounds the \emph{total} number of misses
in all clusters assigned to one bin by $m$. Perron separation converts the
matrix product into cluster gains; a joining argument bounds their product
within each bin by one finite-cluster supremum. Charging each miss
independently would lose this budget-sharing information.

The fixed-language setting has established spectral and multinorm
representations \cite{kozyakin2014,kozyakin2019,philippe2016}. In particular,
Kozyakin--Kuznetsov--Chebotarev \cite[Sections 3.3--3.5]{kozyakin2019}
recodes block-frequency constraints as a finite-type language and obtains
periodic spectral representation. Those results leave the cycle supremum
to be evaluated as the window varies. Weakly-hard control also has scalar
rate abstractions \cite{gaukler2020} and nonlinear sufficient stability
criteria \cite{hertneck2020}; these are not an equality for the present
matrix growth coefficient. Conditional-average variational results
\cite{zhao2014} concern a different constraint.

Rank-one identities and maximum-cycle methods are established
\cite{ahmadi2012,liuxiao2013}. Perron pairings also occur in slow-switching
expansions \cite{monmarche2025}, where retained environmental durations
are stretched under a specified periodic or random law. Here short miss
clusters coexist with a hard adversarial window constraint. Related
hybrid singular-perturbation results \cite{haidar2026} do not directly
supply that packing comparison.

We prove the cluster law, then obtain a quantitative strengthening by
clamping every long gap to a fixed \emph{realizable} gap. The resulting
maximum is attained by a finite word, providing an effective lower witness
as well as an upper enclosure. The Lean development verifies the main
law, the cutoff comparison, both finite-window inequalities, and an
all-later-windows accuracy implication. Section~\ref{sec:formal}
separates imported results, checked statements and classical-only steps.

The scope is fixed strictly positive discrete matrices and fixed miss
budget. We make no claim for arbitrary signed modes, growing budgets,
nonlinear plants or continuous intersample safety, and do not assume that
every matrix pair is realizable by a particular feedback architecture.
The examples explain limitations of the general conclusion. Publication
novelty remains provisional; the unclosed source and human-review gates
are listed in Section~\ref{sec:status}.

\section{The cluster law}

Fix integers $d,m\ge1$ and entrywise strictly positive matrices
$C,D\in\R^{d\times d}$. For integers $k>m$, let
\[
 \Sigma_{m,k}=\left\{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{\Nzero}:
  \sum_{i=t}^{t+k-1}(1-\sigma_i)\le m
  \text{ for every }t\in\Nzero\right\}.
\]
The symbol $1$ denotes success and $0$ denotes a miss. Write $A_1=C$,
$A_0=D$, and define the constrained growth rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \rho_{m,k}=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\ 
 \sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m,k}}
 \left\|A_{\sigma_{N-1}}\cdots A_{\sigma_0}\right\|^{1/N}.
\end{equation}
For definiteness, take the elementwise maximum norm, as in the formal
statement. Standard finite-dimensional norm equivalence shows that any
other fixed matrix norm gives the same rate. Put
\[
 \lambda=\spr(C),\qquad B=C/\lambda,\qquad E=D/\lambda.
\]
Perron--Frobenius theory gives positive vectors $r,\ell$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perron}
 Br=r,\qquad \ell\transpose B=\ell\transpose,\qquad
 \ell\transpose r=1,\qquad B^n\longrightarrow P:=r\ell\transpose.
\end{equation}
For $j\ge2$ and gaps $\boldsymbol g\in\Nzero^{j-1}$ define
\[
 K_j(\boldsymbol g)
  =E B^{g_{j-1}}E\cdots B^{g_1}E,\qquad K_1=E.
\]
Thus $g_1$ is the number of successes after the first miss in chronological
order; matrices multiply in the opposite order. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cluster-values}
 S_j=\sup_{\boldsymbol g\in\Nzero^{j-1}}
       \ell\transpose K_j(\boldsymbol g)r,\qquad
 M_m=\max\{1,S_1,\ldots,S_m\}.
\end{equation}
For $j=1$ the supremum has no gap variables. Each $S_j$ is positive and
finite because the powers of $B$ are bounded and $j$ is fixed.
The supremum need not be attained at finite gaps.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-budget rare-miss law]\label{thm:main}
With $C,D,d,m$ fixed as above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \lim_{k\to\infty}k\log\frac{\rho_{m,k}}{\lambda}=\log M_m.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\log\rho_{m,k}=\log\lambda+k^{-1}\log M_m+o(k^{-1})$.
\end{theorem}

The all-success sequence explains the maximum with $1$ in
\eqref{eq:cluster-values}. In particular, the correction is nonnegative.
The theorem does not identify an exactly maximizing word at each finite
window size.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\subsection{Uniform cluster packing}

Choose an integer $q=q(k)$ with $q\to\infty$ and $q/k\to0$, for instance
$q=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor$. Take $k$ large enough that $mq+1\le k$,
and set $L=k-mq>0$. In a finite prefix of an admissible sequence, join
successive misses into the same cluster whenever fewer than $q$ successes
lie between them.

\begin{lemma}[Window packing]\label{lem:packing}
Each cluster has at most $m$ misses and spans at most $mq$ slots.
Assign a cluster to the integer bin $[tL,(t+1)L-1]$ containing its first
miss. All clusters assigned to one bin contain at most $m$ misses in total.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If one cluster contained $m+1$ misses, the span from the first to the last,
including both endpoints, would be at most
\[m(q-1)+(m+1)=mq+1\le k,\]
contradicting the budget. A cluster of at most $m$ misses spans no more
than $(m-1)q+1\le mq$ slots.

A cluster starting in bin $t$ ends no later than
\[
 (t+1)L-1+(mq-1)=tL+k-2<tL+k.
\]
Consequently every miss in every cluster assigned to bin $t$ belongs
to the same $k$-window $[tL,tL+k-1]$. That window has at most $m$ misses.
If it extends beyond the chosen prefix, use the original infinite
admissible sequence. Truncating the final cluster of a prefix does not
increase its span or count.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Perron separation and joining}

Use the vector norm $\|x\|_r=\max_i |x_i|/r_i$ and its induced matrix norm.
For every nonnegative matrix $W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \|W\|_r=\max_i\frac{(Wr)_i}{r_i}
 \le\kappa\ell\transpose Wr,\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{1}{\min_i\ell_i r_i}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the scalar on the right before multiplication by $\kappa$ is
a weighted average of these ratios with positive weights $\ell_i r_i$
summing to $1$. The induced-norm identity follows from nonnegativity,
with equality attained at $x=r$.

For $q\ge1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \varepsilon_q=\sup_{g\ge q}\ 
  \max_{a,b}\frac{|(B^g-P)_{ab}|}{P_{ab}}.
\end{equation}
All entries of $P$ are positive. By \eqref{eq:perron},
$\varepsilon_q$ is finite, tends to zero, and gives the entrywise bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation}
 B^g\le(1+\varepsilon_q)P\qquad(g\ge q).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Joining cluster gains]\label{lem:joining}
Let $K^{(1)},\ldots,K^{(s)}$ be finite clusters containing a total of $j$
misses. Then
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^s\ell\transpose K^{(i)}r\le S_j.
\]
In particular, the product of gains of any collection with at most $m$
total misses is at most $M_m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Insert $h$ successes between consecutive clusters. For each finite $h$
the result is a $j$-miss cluster, so its gain is at most $S_j$.
Let $h\to\infty$. Every inserted $B^h$ converges to $P$, and
$PWP=(\ell\transpose Wr)P$ factors the limiting gain.
This argument uses a supremum, not attainment of a maximizing gap tuple.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The uniform matrix-product bound}


Let $W$ be a normalized length-$N$ product and let $z$ be its number of
misses. Disjoint full $k$-blocks and one final fragment give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 z\le m\lfloor N/k\rfloor+m.
\end{equation}
The final fragment also has at most $m$ misses because it is contained
in a $k$-window of the infinite extension.

Different clusters are separated by at least $q$ successes. Replace
each such success run using \eqref{eq:separation}. Nonnegative matrix
multiplication preserves entrywise inequalities. In the scalar
$\ell\transpose Wr$, the outer success runs vanish by
\eqref{eq:perron}, and the inserted rank-one separators factor the
remaining expression into cluster gains. There are at most $z$
separators. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:joining} and~\ref{lem:packing}, each
occupied bin contributes at most $M_m$. At most $\lceil N/L\rceil$ bins
are occupied. Thus, including the zero-miss case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:word-upper}
 \|W\|_r\le\kappa(1+\varepsilon_q)^z M_m^{\lceil N/L\rceil}.
\end{equation}
This is a bound on every admissible word.

Fix $k,q$ first. Taking suprema, $N$th roots and the long-product
limit superior in \eqref{eq:word-upper} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-upper}
 \log\frac{\rho_{m,k}}{\lambda}
 \le\frac{\log M_m}{k-mq}+\frac{m}{k}\log(1+\varepsilon_q).
\end{equation}
Multiply by $k$ and then let $k\to\infty$. Since $q/k\to0$ and
$\varepsilon_q\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper-limit}
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}k\log\frac{\rho_{m,k}}{\lambda}
 \le\log M_m.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Matching periodic lower witnesses}

Fix a finite cluster $W$ with $j\le m$ misses, length $a$, and gain
$s=\ell\transpose Wr>0$. Repeat this cluster followed by $k$ successes.
The period is $k+a\ge k$. Residues modulo the period are distinct within
any $k$-window, so that window has at most $j$ misses. The sequence is
therefore admissible. Its period matrix is $Q_k=B^kW$, and
\[
 Q_kr\longrightarrow PWr=sr.
\]
For every $0<t<s$, positivity of $r$ gives $Q_kr\ge tr$ for all sufficiently
large $k$. Nonnegative multiplication then gives $Q_k^Nr\ge t^Nr$.
Using the norm/gain comparison and the repeated-period subsequence in the
rate definition yields
\[
 \rho_{m,k}/\lambda\ge t^{1/(k+a)},\qquad
 \liminf_{k\to\infty}k\log(\rho_{m,k}/\lambda)\ge\log t.
\]
Let $t$ increase to $s$, take the supremum over each fixed finite cluster,
and include the all-success lower bound. The result is $\log M_m$.
Together with the uniform upper bound, this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
No varying maximizing word is interchanged with the $k$-limit, and no
spectral-radius continuity theorem is needed for this argument.

\section{Certified finite-window bounds}\label{sec:quant}

The asymptotic law does not say how large $k$ must be. A two-sided Perron
tail makes the approximation effective. Fix integers $m,q\ge1$ and a
certified $0\le\varepsilon<1$ such that, entrywise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail}
 (1-\varepsilon)P\le B^g\le(1+\varepsilon)P\qquad(g\ge q).
\end{equation}
Put $R=(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)$ and define the genuine finite maximum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 A_m(q)=\max\left\{1,\ \max_{1\le j\le m}\
   \max_{\boldsymbol g\in\{0,\ldots,q\}^{j-1}}
       \ell\transpose K_j(\boldsymbol g)r\right\}.
\end{equation}
Unlike a search containing the ideal projector $P$, every candidate here
is realizable by a finite word. There are
$1+\sum_{j=1}^m(q+1)^{j-1}$ candidates before duplicate values are removed.

\begin{lemma}[Attained cutoff]\label{lem:cutoff}
Under \eqref{eq:two-tail},
\[
 1\le A_m(q)\le M_m\le R^{m-1}A_m(q).
\]
A maximizing word can be chosen with at most $m$ misses and length at most
$m+(m-1)q$, allowing the empty word for the value $1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $g\ge q$, the two tail inequalities imply
$B^g\le(1+\varepsilon)P\le R B^q$. For $g\le q$, nonnegativity and
$R\ge1$ give $B^g\le RB^g$. Clamp each gap to $\min(g,q)$ and multiply
these entrywise inequalities through nonnegative factors. A $j$-miss
cluster has $j-1$ gaps, hence gain at most $R^{j-1}A_m(q)$.
The empty-word contribution is also bounded by $R^{m-1}A_m(q)$.
Taking suprema gives the upper comparison. Every cutoff candidate is
already allowed in $M_m$, giving the lower one. The finite maximum is
attained, and a $j$-miss candidate has length at most $j+(j-1)q$.
\end{proof}

Write $\Xi_{m,k}=k\log(\rho_{m,k}/\lambda)$.
\begin{theorem}[Two-sided finite-window enclosure]\label{thm:finite}
Suppose \eqref{eq:two-tail} holds and $k\ge m(q+1)$. Then
\begin{align}
 \max\{0,\log A_m(q)+\log(1-\varepsilon)\}
 &\le\Xi_{m,k},\label{eq:finite-lower}\\
 \Xi_{m,k}&\le
 \frac{k}{k-mq}\bigl(\log A_m(q)+(m-1)\log R\bigr)
       +m\log(1+\varepsilon).\label{eq:finite-upper}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an attaining word $W$ of length $a\le m+(m-1)q$. The hypothesis
ensures $k-a\ge q$. Repeat $W$ followed by $k-a$ successes. Each
$k$-window is one period and contains at most $m$ misses. Its period
matrix $Q=B^{k-a}W$ satisfies
\[
 Qr\ge(1-\varepsilon)PWr=(1-\varepsilon)A_m(q)r.
\]
Iteration, the norm/gain comparison and the rate definition yield
\eqref{eq:finite-lower}, together with the all-success sequence.
For the upper bound, \eqref{eq:rate-upper} and Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoff}
give \eqref{eq:finite-upper}. In particular, the upper bound applies to
all admissible products, not only the lower witness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Error and a certified accuracy threshold}

Let $H=\log M_m\ge0$. The proof also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
 -\bigl((m-1)\log R-\log(1-\varepsilon)\bigr)
 \le\Xi_{m,k}-H
 \le\frac{mq}{k-mq}H+m\log(1+\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
For $\varepsilon\le1/2$, $k\ge\max\{m(q+1),2mq\}$, and any verified
$\overline H\ge H$, the elementary logarithmic inequalities imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-error}
 -(3m-1)\varepsilon\le\Xi_{m,k}-H
       \le\frac{2mq\,\overline H}{k}+m\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\log(1+\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon$ and
$-\log(1-\varepsilon)\le2\varepsilon$.

Given $\delta>0$, choose $q$ with
$\varepsilon\le\min\{1/2,\delta/(6m)\}$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:k0}
 k_0=\max\left\{m(q+1),\ 2mq,\ 1,\
                 \left\lceil\frac{4mq\,\overline H}{\delta}\right\rceil\right\}.
\end{equation}
Then $|\Xi_{m,k}-H|\le\delta$ for \emph{every} integer $k\ge k_0$.
The lower error is at most $\delta/2$ and the upper error at most
$2\delta/3$. This is a sufficient threshold, not the smallest one.

A computable upper bound on $H$ does not require evaluating logarithms.
If $c\ge1$ and $Er\le cr$, induction gives $M_m\le c^m$, hence
$H\le m(c-1)$. The cutoff supplies another bound,
\[
 H\le A_m(q)-1+(m-1)(R-1).
\]
One may use the smaller of these two bounds. A cutoff used to bound $H$
need not be the cutoff subsequently chosen for the accuracy tolerance.

\subsection{An explicit all-powers tail}

The following classical construction supplies \eqref{eq:two-tail} from
positive normalized data. Set
\[
 \pi_i=\ell_i r_i,\quad \pi_* =\min_i\pi_i,\quad
 \eta=\min_{i,j}\frac{B_{ij}}{P_{ij}},\quad
 \alpha=\eta/2,\quad \theta=1-\alpha.
\]
The fixed-vector identities imply $0<\eta\le1$, since each row of
$B_{ij}/P_{ij}$ has weighted average $1$ with weights $\pi_j$.
Let $T=(B-\alpha P)/\theta$. It is nonnegative, fixes the Perron vectors,
and satisfies $TP=PT=P$. Induction gives
\[
 B^g=P+\theta^g(T^g-P).
\]
From $T^gr=r$ and nonnegativity,
$0\le(T^g)_{ij}/P_{ij}\le1/\pi_j$. Therefore a global relative tail is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-tail}
 \varepsilon_q=\theta^q/\pi_*,\qquad
 \left|\frac{(B^g)_{ij}}{P_{ij}}-1\right|\le\varepsilon_q
                  \quad(g\ge q).
\end{equation}
When $B=P$, the exact bound $\varepsilon_q=0$ is available for $q\ge1$.
For rational normalized matrices and a rational Perron frame, all
minorization identities and the choice of $q$ in \eqref{eq:k0} can be
checked with exact rational arithmetic. General inputs require certified
Perron data; a floating eigensolver is not such a certificate.

\begin{corollary}[Quantitative convergence]\label{cor:quant-rate}
For each fixed positive pair and fixed $m$,
\[
 k\log(\rho_{m,k}/\lambda)=\log M_m+O(\log k/k).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $c_*=1/\pi_*$ and $b=-\log\theta>0$. Take
$q=\max\{1,\lceil\log(c_*k)/b\rceil\}$, so
$\varepsilon_q\le1/k$ and $q=O(\log k)$.
For all sufficiently large $k$, both $k\ge m(q+1)$ and $k\ge2mq$ hold.
Equation~\eqref{eq:simple-error} with fixed $\overline H$ gives the result.
The constants depend on the fixed matrices and $m$.
\end{proof}
We do not claim that the logarithmic factor or these constants are optimal.

\subsection{Rational stability tests}

Put $A=A_m(q)$, $U=AR^{m-1}$ and $L=k-mq$. Exponentiating the finite-window
bounds gives
\[
 \rho_{m,k}^{kL}\le\lambda^{kL}U^k(1+\varepsilon)^{mL},\qquad
 \rho_{m,k}^{k}\ge\lambda^k\max\{1,(1-\varepsilon)A\}.
\]
Thus a first right-hand side below $1$ certifies exponential stability;
a second right-hand side at least $1$ excludes it. For rational data these
comparisons are exact integer-power calculations. Otherwise the result
may be inconclusive. These tests do not claim that the period-$k$ witness
is optimal or that the certified window is minimal.

\section{Gap structure and its limitations}

In dimension two, write the second eigenvalue of $B$ as $\mu$, where
$|\mu|<1$. Then $B^g=P+\mu^g(I-P)$, and, with
$a=\ell\transpose Er$ and $b_g=\ell\transpose EB^gEr$,
\[
 b_g=a^2+\beta\mu^g,\qquad
 \beta=\ell\transpose E(I-P)Er.
\]
The positive and negative cases for $\mu$ show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar}
 M_2=\max\{1,a^2,b_0,b_1\}.
\end{equation}
For $\mu=0$, the zeroth power is interpreted separately as $B^0=I$.
The finite list in \eqref{eq:planar} is not dimension independent.

\begin{proposition}[Prescribed optimal pair gap]\label{prop:gap}
For every integer $t\ge1$ there is a strictly positive pair in dimension
$n=t+1$ for which $b_g$ is uniquely maximized, over all finite nonnegative
gaps, at $g=t$, and its value exceeds the infinite-gap limit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Index coordinates by $0,\ldots,n-1$. Let $R_{i,i+1\bmod n}=1$ be the
cyclic permutation matrix and let $P=\ones\ones\transpose/n$. Set
\[
 B=\tfrac12(R+P),\quad v=\ones+n e_0,\quad
 w=\ones+n e_1,\quad E=vw\transpose/(2n).
\]
Take $r=\ones$ and $\ell=\ones/n$. All entries are positive and
$a=2$. Since $R^g e_0=e_{-g\bmod n}$ and
$B^g=P+2^{-g}(R^g-P)$, direct multiplication gives
\[
 b_g=4+2^{-g}\bigl(n\mathbf1_{\{g\equiv t\pmod n\}}-1\bigr).
\]
Outside the residue class $t$, this is less than $4$. Inside that
class it is greater than $4$ and strictly decreases with $g$.
The unique maximum is therefore $b_t=4+t/2^t>4$.
\end{proof}

Choosing $t$ outside any proposed fixed finite list defeats that list.
This does not exclude dimension-dependent bounds or adaptive truncation,
and it is not a statement about exact finite-$k$ maximizing words.

\section{A reset application at finite window sizes}\label{sec:reset}

The following elementary application of rank-one factorization
\cite{ahmadi2012} separates the asymptotic coefficient from finite-window
optimality. It is not asserted as an independent novelty claim.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one success mode]\label{prop:reset}
Assume $B=P=r\ell\transpose$ and set $e_j=\ell\transpose E^j r$.
If $e_1\le1$, $e_2\ge1$ and $e_3\le e_2$, then for $k\ge4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset}
 \rho_{2,k}=\lambda e_2^{1/k},\qquad
 \rho_{3,k}=\lambda e_2^{1/(k-1)},\qquad M_2=M_3=e_2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every nonempty success run equals $P$. Thus $\ell\transpose Wr$
factors into the values $e_j$ of the consecutive miss runs, including
boundary runs. Under budget three these runs have length at most three.
Delete single misses, and delete one endpoint of each triple. This
preserves admissibility and does not decrease the scalar gain, since
$e_1\le1$ and $e_3\le e_2$. The remaining runs are pairs.
Their starts are at least $k-1$ slots apart, or four misses would fit
inside one $k$-window. At most $N/(k-1)+1$ pairs remain in $N$ slots.
Equation~\eqref{eq:norm} proves the upper rate in the second formula.
Repeating two misses followed by $k-3$ successes has period $k-1$,
is admissible, and has normalized period spectral radius $e_2$.
It attains the matching lower bound.

For budget two, delete single misses. Pair starts must be at least
$k$ slots apart: otherwise the last miss of one pair and both misses
of the next fit in a $k$-window. The same bound and a pair repeated
every $k$ slots give the first formula. Finally
$S_2=\max(e_2,e_1^2)=e_2$ and
$S_3=\max(e_3,e_1e_2,e_1^3)\le e_2$, proving the last equality.
\end{proof}

For example, take
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}99/100&1/100\\99/100&1/100\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad E=\begin{pmatrix}1/10&1/10\\20&1/10\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here $r=\ones$, $\ell\transpose=(99/100,1/100)$, and
\[
 e_1=399/1000,\qquad e_2=10349/5000,\qquad e_3=120797/100000.
\]
Set $C=(9/10)B$ and $D=(9/10)E$. Under budget three, uniform
exponential stability is equivalent to $\lambda^{k-1}e_2<1$.
At $k=7$ and $k=8$ respectively, the exact comparisons are
\[
 \frac{5499882909}{5000000000}>1,\qquad
 \frac{49498946181}{50000000000}<1.
\]
Thus eight is the first stable window among $k\ge4$. The maximizing
period is $k-1$. When $e_2>1$, the two budgets have different finite-$k$
rates despite identical first-order coefficients.

\section{Formal statement and verification scope}\label{sec:formal}

The accompanying Lean 4.32.2 development builds on the pinned mathlib
library \cite{mathlib2020}. The declaration
\texttt{MissedUpdates.Matrices.rare\_miss\_cluster\_law}
takes strictly positive real matrices on a nonempty finite index type,

The formal norm is the elementwise maximum norm. Its two-sided comparison
with the Perron gain is proved with constants independent of the word.
The ordinary spectral radius is defined using the complex spectrum of the
complexified real matrix; its equality to the positive normalizing eigenvalue
is proved before converting it to a real number. Nonempty/bounded supremum
sets and the necessary limsup boundedness are established explicitly.
Gap order is reversed between chronological words and left-to-right factors,
and the formal product identity accounts for this reversal.

The combinatorial proof uses the sufficient occupied-bin count
$\lfloor N/(k-mq)\rfloor+1$ in place of the ceiling used above. The extra
constant vanishes at the long-product rate. For the upper limit, the formal
argument fixes a tail tolerance, lets $k$ grow, and then shrinks the tolerance.
The lower argument uses the period $k+a$ construction above. These are
proof choices, not additional hypotheses on the model.

Perron eigenvector existence and finite-chain Dobrushin convergence are
imported from the pinned HNBM source \cite{hnbm}, not claimed as new results.

20 files remain byte-identical to the recorded upstream commit, with their
original notices retained. This project supplies the normalization bridge,
canonical word/matrix assembly, rate comparison and limit proof.

\section{Verification and publication status}\label{sec:status}

The accompanying standard-library Python checker uses exact rational
arithmetic for prescribed fixtures. It checks 408 admissible-word
matrix/packing cases and 7,767 reset run-replacement cases, rejects three
deliberately false assertions, and verifies finite instances of the
prescribed-gap identities and coefficient enclosures. These finite
checks support the calculations; they do not prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The all-parameter argument is the classical
proof above. The checker is fixture-specific, not a general validator
for arbitrary matrix inputs.

The formal verification scope is specified in Section~\ref{sec:formal}.
The quantitative tool additionally checks exact rational tail identities,
attained finite-cutoff values, conservative stability classifications and
accuracy certificates. These are not tests of previously unknown matrix
trajectories in place of proof.

The manuscript, code and proof scripts used AI assistance. Kernel checking
does not establish novelty or replace author accountability. Authorship,
affiliations, a fresh reader assessment and human specialist review remain
unresolved. No reviewer has been appointed and no manuscript has been sent.

A targeted prior-art audit found no direct match in the inspected
theorem statements, but this is not evidence sufficient to establish
priority. Important original texts remained unavailable, including
Zhao's 2019 work on constrained ergodic optimization for asymptotically
additive potentials, Hertneck--Linsenmayer--Allgower's 2021 weakly-hard
control paper, and the Gurvits--Samorodnitsky 2005 original attributed
in the revision appendix of \cite{ahmadi2012}. No result is attributed
directly to an unread text. The exact 2019 Kozyakin--Kuznetsov--Chebotarev survey has now been read at
its relevant statements and is cited directly. The earlier preprints
\cite{zhao2014,hertneck2020} were inspected separately, without identifying
them with unread journal versions. The review materials retain the remaining
access gaps and distinguish proposed reviewers from received assessments.
